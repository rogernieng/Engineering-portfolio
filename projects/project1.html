G-Class Model Rocket

Overview

Co-engineered with a team of five to design, fabricate, simulate, and launch a G-Class model rocket. This introductory aerospace engineering project provided hands-on experience with CAD, aerodynamic stability, flight simulation, 3D printing, fabrication, and rocket assembly.

The final rocket measured approximately 64.6 cm in length, 4.1 cm in diameter, and weighed 526 grams.

Project Goals

  • Design and develop a model rocket from the ground up.
  • Use CAD to create and refine the rocket's components.
  • Simulate the rocket's flight performance before launch.
  • Manufacture components using 3D printing and other fabrication methods.
  • Assemble and test the rocket as a five-person engineering team.
  • Prepare the completed rocket for a successful launch.

Design & Engineering

The rocket was developed through an iterative design process involving CAD modeling, flight simulation, fabrication, and physical assembly.

  • Developed rocket components using SOLIDWORKS CAD.
  • Used OpenRocket to evaluate flight performance and aerodynamic stability.
  • Considered center of mass and aerodynamic stability during the design process.
  • Iterated component designs based on simulation results and manufacturing constraints.
  • Fabricated components using 3D printing and assembled the final rocket for flight.

Key Specifications

  • Rocket Length: 64.6 cm
  • Rocket Diameter: 4.1 cm
  • Rocket Mass: 526 g
  • Motor Class: G-Class
  • Team Size: 5 people

Tools & Technologies

  • SOLIDWORKS: CAD modeling and component design
  • OpenRocket: Flight simulation and stability analysis
  • 3D Printing: Rapid prototyping and component fabrication
  • CAD: Design iteration and dimensional development
  • Rocketry: Rocket assembly, testing, and launch preparation

Challenges

  • Balanced aerodynamic stability with overall rocket mass.
  • Designed components while considering real-world manufacturing constraints.
  • Iterated the design based on OpenRocket simulation results.
  • Coordinated component design and assembly between five team members.
  • Used simulation to identify potential design issues before physical fabrication.

What I Learned

  • Engineering Iteration: Learned to refine designs based on simulation results rather than relying solely on the initial CAD model.
  • Aerodynamic Stability: Developed a practical understanding of how center of mass and aerodynamic characteristics affect rocket stability.
  • CAD & Manufacturing: Learned how to design components while considering actual fabrication constraints.
  • Simulation: Gained experience using OpenRocket to evaluate expected flight performance before launch.
  • Team Engineering: Learned how to divide design responsibilities and coordinate components within a larger engineering system.
  • Testing: Experienced the importance of validating an engineering design through both simulation and physical testing.

Results

  • Completed the design and fabrication of a G-Class model rocket.
  • Used OpenRocket simulation to evaluate the rocket's expected flight performance and stability.
  • Successfully assembled the rocket and prepared it for launch.
  • Gained hands-on experience taking an engineering concept from CAD and simulation through fabrication and testing.
